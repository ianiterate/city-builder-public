# Traits and events

## The lord's traits

Each lord draws two traits at the crowning. They multiply together, so a greedy and kindly lord is
a little of both.

| Trait | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Just | tax unhappiness halved |
| Greedy | half again as much tax gold, half again as much resentment |
| Pious | civic happiness bonuses times 1.5 |
| Warlike | militia strength times 1.5, in raids and campaigns |
| Kindly | tax unhappiness times 0.75, tax gold times 0.8 |

The relic of Saint Cuthwin, an event, can make a lord pious outright.

## Events

Roughly one day in twenty, when nothing else is pending and at least ten days since the last, the
steward brings a matter to the hall with two answers. An unanswered event takes its first answer
after five days. Every event has conditions, so the crofters do not beg for wood in summer and no
envoy comes under a white rag unless there is a war. Most can repeat after a cooldown; the relic
comes once. Difficulty scales how often they come.

| Event | The choice |
| --- | --- |
| The crofters beg for wood | give 15 firewood for goodwill, or refuse and annoy the houses |
| The river is over the bank | lose a little grain and some patience on the dikes, or lose 30 grain |
| No rain since the sowing | pay 30 gold and 10 planks to dig, or ration grain |
| Sickness in the cooper's house | pay 45 gold for a physician, or seal the house and lose three people |
| A healer on the road | keep her for bread and gold, or send her on |
| The merchant's locked cart | 60 gold for 60 meat, or haggle for less and annoy everyone |
| A demand of tribute | pay 50 gold, or refuse and face a double raid |
| The Hill Folk send a token | answer with 30 grain, or with words |
| Refugees at the gate | turn them away, or admit three settlers for 10 bread |
| The village asks for a feast | keep quiet, or pay 35 gold and food for a big lift in spirits |
| A claim against the regency | buy the claimant off for 60 gold, or raise taxes to 3 and mourn longer |
| The relic of Saint Cuthwin | 70 gold for a pious lord, or refuse |
| Men on the wood road | pay a 30 gold toll, or lose 30 wood and 4 tools |
| Wolves in the fold | arm the herders with 8 tools, or lose one villager and gain some meat |
| Swallows early in the eaves | spend 20 gold for a big sowing, or trust the omen for a small one |
| The poor box is full | feed the poor, or take 35 gold and their goodwill |
| The man who says he is kin | welcome him for 25 gold and a settler, or drive him off for 15 |
| Poachers in the lord's wood | pardon them, or hang them for meat and the worst happiness hit in the game |
| The boundary stone | pay 30 gold for a stronger claim, or refuse |
| A company for hire | 40 gold for eight spears on your next campaign, or send them to your enemy |
| An envoy under a white rag | 30 grain for a 120-day truce, or no terms |
| Sickness across the water | send bread to the strongest house, or shut the road and let them shrink |
| Men off the road | take two deserters, or turn them back to their army |
| Foragers in the fields | let them take grain, or post men for gold |
| A quarrel in the weakest house | stay out, or back the usurper for 60 gold and a claim on their land |
| What the valley remembers | a feast for every house, or a colder remembrance and a claim |
| Desperate stragglers | take in three settlers with 45 grain and 10 firewood, or keep only the sacks |

Costs that cannot be paid are taken in happiness instead. Some costs scale with the years played or
with your standing, so the same matter weighs more on a great house.

**Desperate stragglers** is the one matter that is never rolled. It arrives when a settlement falls
under four adults: word of an emptying hamlet travels, and three ragged travellers stand at the
door with two sacks of grain and a bundle of firewood, asking for a roof. The settlers and the
goods land in that settlement, not at the capital. A village founded less than 60 days ago is
left to fill on its own. It can happen again, but at most once every 240 days, so it is a hand up
out of a disaster and not a way to grow a village.
